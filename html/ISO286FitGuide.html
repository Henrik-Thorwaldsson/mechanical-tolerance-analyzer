ISO 286 Fit Guide

Start with the required function of the hole and shaft.

The suggested fits below are practical starting points for engineering selection.
Clearance fit
The shaft is always smaller than the hole. Clearance is always present between the mating parts.
Typical applications
  • Sliding guides and locating pilots
  • Journal bearings and rotating shafts
  • Pulleys and freely moving components
  • Parts requiring easy assembly and removal
  • Machinery where dirt, temperature variation or slight misalignment may occur
Transition fit
The tolerance zones overlap. The actual manufactured parts may therefore have either a small clearance or a small interference.
Typical applications
  • Dowel pins and locating features
  • Accurately positioned removable parts
  • Precision hubs and gears
  • Couplings requiring accurate alignment
  • Assemblies that may require light tapping or pressing
Interference fit
The shaft is larger than the hole. Assembly normally requires pressing, heating or cooling.
Typical applications
  • Bearing inner races on shafts
  • Bearing seats
  • Press-fit locating pins
  • Gear hubs and couplings
  • Permanent or semi-permanent joints

Useful fits to remember
H7/g6 — Sliding fit
Accurate guided movement with small clearance.

H7/k6 — Locational transition fit
Accurate location with a snug assembly.

H7/p6 — Locational interference fit
Light press fit where relative movement is not intended.

These three cover many common general mechanical design situations and are useful starting points before selecting a more specialised fit.

Understanding ISO 286 tolerance classes
Example:
H7 / g6
Uppercase letter = hole
H7

Lowercase letter = shaft
g6

The letter defines the position of the tolerance zone relative to nominal size.

The number defines the IT grade and therefore the width of the tolerance zone.

Lower IT number → tighter tolerance → generally higher manufacturing and inspection effort.
Tighter is not automatically better.

Use the loosest tolerance that reliably fulfils the functional requirement.
Manufacturing cost — rough rule of thumb
IT grade Relative cost Typical manufacturing level
IT11 ≈ 1× Rough machining, drilling and non-critical dimensions
IT9 ≈ 2× Standard controlled CNC machining
IT7 ≈ 4× Precision turning, boring or reaming
IT6 ≈ 8× High precision machining, grinding or closely controlled processes
IT5 ≈ 16× Very high precision, grinding, honing or specialist processes
LOOSER   IT11 → IT9 → IT7 → IT6 → IT5   TIGHTER
Cost note: These multipliers are approximate relative manufacturing-cost rules of thumb only. They are not quotations.

Actual cost depends on size, material, geometry, surface finish, process, machine capability, inspection, production volume and supplier capability.
